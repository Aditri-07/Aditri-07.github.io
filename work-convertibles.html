Convertible Bond Credit Treatment

Binomial latticeTsiveriotis–FernandesCredit spreadGreeksPython

A convertible is a bond and an option on the issuer's equity in one security, and the two halves carry different default risk. This project prices vanilla U.S. convertibles on a recombining binomial lattice under two credit treatments and measures where, and by how much, they disagree. The lattice below is the repository's pricer ported to your browser; at the base case it reproduces the repo's numbers to the cent.

At every node the holder takes the larger of converting (ratio × share price) and holding. Holding is worth the discounted expected value of the next step plus any coupon. The question is what discount rate "discounted" means.

The lattice

Inside the tree

Each node is a share price on the Cox–Ross–Rubinstein tree, u = eσ√Δt, d = 1/u, risk-neutral p = (erΔt − d)/(u − d). Under Tsiveriotis–Fernandes each node carries two values: the cash-only part (COCB) owed by the issuer, and the equity-linked part (COTV). The ring shows the split. Hover or tap a node.

Model

Display tree: 10 steps of 6 months over the bond's life, so you can see individual decisions. All prices elsewhere on this page use 300 steps.

Backward induction

Two discount rates, one decision

  1. At maturity, convert if it beats redemption:COTV = c·S_T if c·S_T > F + cpn, else COCB = F + cpn
  2. Step back. Cash the issuer owes is discounted with credit; equity the holder would receive is not:COCB = e^(−(r+s)Δt)·E[COCB′] + cpn
    COTV = e^(−rΔt)·E[COTV′]
  3. If converting now is worth more than holding, all value moves to the equity leg:if c·S > COCB + COTV: COTV = c·S, COCB = 0
  4. Price = COCB + COTV at the root. The single-rate model skips the split and discounts everything with credit:V = max(c·S, e^(−(r+s)Δt)·E[V′] + cpn)
Pricing divergence

Where the credit treatment matters

Deep out of the money, both models price a risky bond. Deep in the money, both price equity. In between, the single rate charges credit on value that will most likely be paid in shares. The gap peaks past the at-the-money point, because the chance of not converting persists well beyond the par crossover at this maturity and volatility.

The repository's coarser sweep reports 8.50% at S₀ = 70; on this finer $2.50 grid the peak reads 8.6% at S₀ = 75. Binomial prices oscillate by a few tenths of a point between neighbouring spots (nodes shift relative to the conversion boundary), so read the peak as ≈ 8.5% around S₀ = 70–80.

Risk

Delta and CS01 by moneyness

Delta by central finite difference (±0.50 in S₀); CS01 as the price change for a +100 bp spread bump. Deep in the money, the single-rate CS01 is exactly zero because conversion wins at every node, while TF keeps a small residual sensitivity from paths that never convert.

Recomputed live at 300 steps for the current spread and volatility. At the base case this matches cs01_sensitivity.csv in the repository.

Scope

Known simplifications

  • Flat credit spread and flat volatility: no term structure, no skew.
  • American conversion only; no issuer call or holder put, which real convertibles usually have and which would cap the upside.
  • Coupons mapped to the nearest lattice step rather than exact day counts.
  • The stock follows a risk-free drift and does not jump on default; only discounting differs between treatments, the standard TF simplification.
  • Magnitudes are specific to the base case; the shape of the divergence curve is the result.
