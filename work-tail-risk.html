Estimator 1 · efficient

Importance sampling: precise, but aimed at the model

  1. Shift the sampling distribution into the tail by exponential tilting:N(μ − θ, σ²), θ = −σ·z₁₋α
  2. Reweight every draw by the likelihood ratio so the estimate stays unbiased under the model:w(x) = φ(x) / φθ(x)
  3. Find VaR by bracketing and bisection on the weighted tail probability:p̂IS(x) = (1/N) Σ wᵢ·1{Lᵢ > x} = 1 − α

Estimator 2 · robust

Discrete moment matching: a bracket instead of a point

  1. Put the loss on a grid of support points:X₀ < X₁ < … < Xₘ
  2. Treat the probability on each point as an unknown:pⱼ ≥ 0, Σ pⱼ = 1
  3. Only require the first d moments to match:Σ pⱼ Xⱼᵏ = mₖ, k = 1…d
  4. Solve two linear programs at every x for the lowest and highest possible CDF, then read VaR off both envelopes:min / max Σ_{Xⱼ ≤ x} pⱼ

The shaded band holds every tail probability any distribution with these d moments can have. Few moments give a wide, conservative bracket; more moments tighten it toward the Gaussian, and at some point the bracket can stop containing the truth.

The worst case behind the upper bound: a discrete distribution with exactly the Gaussian's first d moments that parks just over 1 − α of its mass at the upper bound. Mass on a log scale. Computed live: 201-point grid on [−8σ, 12σ], moments of the calibrated Gaussian, dense two-phase simplex.
